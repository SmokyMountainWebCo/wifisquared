How Much Internet Speed Does Your Family Actually Need?

By the Wifi Squared team · June 30, 2026 · 4 min read

Internet providers love to sell speed the way truck commercials sell horsepower — more is always better, right? The truth: most families are either paying for speed they never touch, or bottlenecked by a plan they outgrew. Here's how to figure out where you land.

What common activities actually use

ActivitySpeed per device/stream
Browsing, email, social media1–5 Mbps
Music streaming1–2 Mbps
Video call (Zoom, FaceTime)3–5 Mbps
HD video streaming5–8 Mbps
4K video streaming15–25 Mbps
Online gaming3–10 Mbps (latency matters more)
Security cameras / smart home1–4 Mbps each, running constantly

The trick is that these stack. Speed is like water pressure: one shower running is fine, but the question is what happens when the dishwasher, washing machine, and a second shower all kick on at once.

A quick rule of thumb

  • Up to 18 Mbps — one or two people, everyday streaming and browsing, up to 4 devices. That's Essential Connect at $60/mo.
  • Up to 50 Mbps — the sweet spot for most families: HD streaming on multiple TVs, work-from-home video calls, kids online at the same time. One of our customers runs four TVs at once on this tier with no issues. That's Standard Connect at $75/mo.
  • Up to 100 Mbps — 4K streaming, serious gaming, big households, lots of smart-home gear. That's Performance Connect at $97/mo.
  • Up to 500 Mbps — large households, several remote workers at once, heavy uploads and cloud backups. That's Ultra Connect at $107/mo — or Fiber Connect at $75/mo if fiber has reached your community.

All of those are month-to-month with no data caps. A one-time $150 installation and equipment fee applies to every plan.

The thing that matters more than speed for gamers

If someone in your house games online, latency — the delay before data starts moving — matters more than raw speed. A 25 Mbps connection with low latency beats a 200 Mbps connection with high latency every single time. This is exactly where fixed wireless shines over traditional satellite: our signal travels a few miles to a local tower, not to orbit and back.

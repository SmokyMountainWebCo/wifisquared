Fixed Wireless vs. Satellite vs. Starlink: What's Best for Rural East Tennessee?

By the Wifi Squared team · July 14, 2026 · 5 min read

If you live in the mountains or out in the county, you've probably been told "service isn't available at your address" more than once. Here's an honest look at the three options that actually work out here — including where each one wins.

Why the distance matters

Every internet connection is a round trip: your click goes out, the answer comes back. With traditional satellite, that trip goes to geostationary orbit and back — tens of thousands of miles each way. No amount of speed fixes that delay, which is why video calls stutter and online games feel unplayable on old-school satellite.

Fixed wireless keeps the whole trip local. Your antenna talks to a tower a few miles away, and from there the traffic rides the same backbone as everyone else's. That's why a 50 Mbps fixed wireless connection often feels faster than a "faster" satellite plan.

What about Starlink?

Credit where it's due: Starlink solved satellite's latency problem, and if you're in a spot with no tower coverage at all, it's a genuine option. But for most families it's the expensive route — roughly $120/month plus a few hundred dollars in equipment, with support handled through an app instead of a person.

Our take: if we have line-of-sight to your property, fixed wireless gives you comparable real-world performance at a lower monthly cost, with a local human on the other end of the phone when something acts up. If we don't cover your area yet, Starlink beats old-school satellite every time — and you can always check back with us, because new towers go where the demand is.

The weather question

Everyone out here asks it, and fair enough. Traditional satellite suffers from "rain fade" because the signal punches through the entire atmosphere. A fixed wireless signal only travels to a nearby ridge, so everyday East Tennessee rain and cloud cover barely touch it. In a severe storm you might see a brief slowdown — and afterward, a local tech makes sure everything's dialed back in.

The bottom line

  • Have line-of-sight to one of our towers? Fixed wireless is the best mix of speed, price, latency, and support.
  • No tower coverage at your address? Starlink over traditional satellite, no contest.
